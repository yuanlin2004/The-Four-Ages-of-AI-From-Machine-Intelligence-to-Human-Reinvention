# References and Further Reading

This page collects the sources behind **The Four Ages of AI**. They are grouped by the age whose claims they support, and each entry is labeled by the **kind of evidence** it offers, so readers can see how much weight it can bear:

- **Empirical** — a peer-reviewed or working-paper study reporting data. The strongest support here, though single studies can fail to replicate.
- **Primary technical** — an original technical paper describing a method or system.
- **History / analogy** — a historical account that illuminates a mechanism but cannot establish AI's trajectory.
- **Theory / philosophy** — a framework for interpretation, cited as an interlocutor rather than an endorsement of this essay's thesis.
- **Policy / framework** — a law, standard or institutional report.
- **Speculative synthesis** — a forward-looking argument about possible futures.

The four-age framework, the illustrative scenes, the dates attached to each age and the judgments about what ought to happen are **the essay's own interpretation and hypotheses**. No single source establishes them.

## Age I · The Mirror {#mirror}

- **Empirical** · **Nightingale & Farid (2022).** [*AI-synthesized faces are indistinguishable from real faces and more trustworthy*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2120481119). PNAS 119(8). Participants classified synthetic and real faces at about chance and rated synthetic faces as slightly more trustworthy.
- **Empirical** · **Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman (2023).** [*Human heuristics for AI-generated language are flawed*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2208839120). PNAS 120(11). People could not reliably identify AI-written self-presentations and relied on cues that language models easily reproduce.
- **Empirical** · **Doshi & Hauser (2024).** [*Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content*](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290). Science Advances 10(28). One experiment with short stories; supports a possible homogenization mechanism, not a general law.
- **Primary technical / history** · **Weizenbaum (1966).** [*ELIZA — a computer program for the study of natural language communication between man and machine*](https://doi.org/10.1145/365153.365168). Communications of the ACM 9(1).
- **Theory / philosophy** · **Weizenbaum (1976).** *Computer Power and Human Reason: From Judgment to Calculation.* W. H. Freeman. Weizenbaum's reflections on users attributing understanding to ELIZA.
- **Empirical (qualitative)** · **Bishop (2019).** *Managing visibility on YouTube through algorithmic gossip.* New Media & Society 21(11–12). How creators theorize and adapt to recommendation algorithms.
- **Empirical (qualitative)** · **Cotter (2019).** *Playing the visibility game: How digital influencers and algorithms negotiate influence on Instagram.* New Media & Society 21(4).
- **Theory / philosophy** · **Goffman (1959).** *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life.* Anchor Books (first edition 1956).
- **Theory / philosophy** · **Foucault (1969).** "What Is an Author?" Lecture to the Société française de philosophie; English translation in *Language, Counter-Memory, Practice* (1977).
- **Theory / philosophy** · **Benjamin (1935).** "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction." First published in French in 1936. On reproduction and the loss of an artwork's "aura".
- **History / analogy** · **Confucius.** *Analects* 7.1 ("I transmit but do not innovate"; translations vary). An example of a tradition that values faithful transmission over originality.

## Age II · The Surrender {#surrender}

- **Theory / empirical** · **Bainbridge (1983).** [*Ironies of automation*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8). Automatica 19(6). Why automating routine work can leave humans less able to handle failures.
- **Empirical review** · **Parasuraman & Riley (1997).** [*Humans and automation: Use, misuse, disuse, abuse*](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886). Human Factors 39(2).
- **Empirical review** · **Parasuraman & Manzey (2010).** [*Complacency and bias in human use of automation: An attentional integration*](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720810376055). Human Factors 52(3).
- **Empirical** · **Dahmani & Bohbot (2020).** [*Habitual use of GPS negatively impacts spatial memory during self-guided navigation*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7156656/). Scientific Reports 10. Cross-sectional plus a small three-year follow-up.
- **Empirical (survey)** · **Lee et al. (2025).** [*The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778). CHI 2025. Self-reported; treated in the essay as a hypothesis, not a finding of cognitive decline.
- **Empirical** · **Sparrow, Liu & Wegner (2011).** [*Google effects on memory: Cognitive consequences of having information at our fingertips*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1207745). Science 333(6043).
- **Empirical (replication)** · **Camerer et al. (2018).** [*Evaluating the replicability of social science experiments in Nature and Science between 2010 and 2015*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0399-z). Nature Human Behaviour 2. Includes a failed replication of the effect tested from Sparrow et al. (2011).
- **Empirical** · **Johnson & Goldstein (2003).** [*Do defaults save lives?*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1091721) Science 302(5649). Organ-donation defaults and consent rates.
- **Theory** · **Thaler & Sunstein (2008).** *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness.* Yale University Press.
- **Theory / philosophy** · **Zuboff (2019).** *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism.* PublicAffairs.
- **Theory / philosophy** · **Illich (1973).** *Tools for Conviviality.* Harper & Row.
- **Policy / framework** · **NIST (2023).** [*Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1).

## Age III · The Irrelevance {#irrelevance}

- **Empirical (historical)** · **Jahoda, Lazarsfeld & Zeisel (1933).** *Die Arbeitslosen von Marienthal.* English edition: *Marienthal: The Sociography of an Unemployed Community* (1971).

- **Empirical** · **Case & Deaton (2020).** *Deaths of Despair and the Future of Capitalism.* Princeton University Press.
- **Theory / philosophy** · **Sandel (2020).** *The Tyranny of Merit: What's Become of the Common Good?* Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
- **Empirical (exposure estimate)** · **Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin & Rock (2024).** *GPTs are GPTs: Labor market impact potential of LLMs.* Science 384(6702); [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130). Measures task exposure, not job loss.
- **Empirical** · **Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (2025).** [*Generative AI at Work*](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044). Quarterly Journal of Economics 140(2). Earlier version: NBER Working Paper 31161 (2023).
- **Empirical** · **Noy & Zhang (2023).** [*Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586). Science 381(6654).
- **Empirical** · **Autor, Chin, Salomons & Seegmiller (2024).** [*New Frontiers: The Origins and Content of New Work, 1940–2018*](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30389). Quarterly Journal of Economics 139(3).
- **Empirical / theory** · **Acemoglu & Restrepo (2019).** [*Automation and New Tasks: How Technology Displaces and Reinstates Labor*](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.33.2.3). Journal of Economic Perspectives 33(2).
- **Empirical / history** · **Autor (2015).** [*Why Are There Still So Many Jobs? The History and Future of Workplace Automation*](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.29.3.3). Journal of Economic Perspectives 29(3).
- **Policy / framework** · **Royal College of Radiologists (2023).** [*Clinical radiology workforce census 2023*](https://www.rcr.ac.uk/news-policy/latest-updates/2023-clinical-radiology-and-clinical-oncology-workforce-census-reports/). Reports a continuing shortfall of radiologists in the UK.
- **Empirical** · **Vivalt, Rhodes, Bartik, Broockman, Krause & Miller (2024).** [*The Employment Effects of a Guaranteed Income: Experimental Evidence from Two U.S. States*](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32719). NBER Working Paper 32719.
- **History / anthropology** · **Suzman (2020).** *Work: A Deep History, from the Stone Age to the Age of Robots.* Bloomsbury / Penguin Press.
- **History / anthropology** · **Sahlins (1972).** "The Original Affluent Society", in *Stone Age Economics.* Aldine. Influential and contested.

- **Theory / empirical** · **Graeber (2018).** *Bullshit Jobs: A Theory.* Simon & Schuster.
- **Speculative synthesis** · **Keynes (1930).** "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren." Reprinted in *Essays in Persuasion* (1931).

## Technical companions {#technical}

These references support the [technical companions](technical/index.md), which preserve the essay's original account of the machine-side history of AI.

### Architecture and the Transformer transition

- **Primary technical** · **Vaswani et al. (2017).** [*Attention Is All You Need*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762). Introduced the Transformer; foundational to the companion's account of architectural convergence and parallelizable sequence modeling.
- **Primary technical** · **Sutskever, Vinyals & Le (2014).** [*Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3215). Illustrates recurrent encoder–decoder sequence learning preceding the Transformer.
- **Primary technical** · **He et al. (2015).** [*Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385). An influential example of specialized deep learning architecture in computer vision.
- **Primary technical** · **Goodfellow et al. (2014).** [*Generative Adversarial Nets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661). A representative generative modeling approach from the architecturally diverse era.

### LLMs, scaling and parallel computing

- **Primary technical** · **Brown et al. (2020).** [*Language Models are Few-Shot Learners*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165). In-context learning and adaptation without conventional per-task training.
- **Primary technical** · **Kaplan et al. (2020).** [*Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361). Empirical relationships among language modeling performance, parameters, data and training compute.
- **Primary technical** · **Hoffmann et al. (2022).** [*Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556). Reexamines how model parameters and training tokens should scale under a fixed compute budget.
- **Primary technical** · **Dao et al. (2022).** [*FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135). Central to the companion's discussion of tiling, block-oriented computation and GPU memory reads and writes.
- **Primary technical** · **Shazeer et al. (2017).** [*Outrageously Large Neural Networks: The Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts Layer*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06538). Foundational work on conditional computation and sparse expert activation.
- **Primary technical** · **Yao et al. (2022).** [*ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629). Research on interleaving reasoning traces and tool actions.

### Agentic systems, tools and reliability

- **Primary technical** · **Schick et al. (2023).** [*Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761). A research example of language models selecting and using external tools.
- **Primary technical** · **Shinn et al. (2023).** [*Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366). Explores feedback, memory and improved action trajectories.
- **Policy / framework** · **NIST (2023).** [*Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1). A framework for governance and management of AI-related risk.
- **Policy / framework** · **OWASP.** [*Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications*](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/). Practical security risks including prompt injection, inappropriate tool access and related application-layer challenges.

These references illuminate agentic capabilities and the need for governance. They do **not** independently prove the companion's forecasts about SaaS disruption, personal hardware or future economic institutions.

### AI for science, mathematics and quantum computation

- **Primary technical** · **Romera-Paredes et al. (2023).** [*Mathematical Discoveries from Program Search with Large Language Models*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06924-6). FunSearch; illustrates LLM-guided discovery coupled with automated evaluation.
- **Primary technical** · **Trinh et al. (2024).** [*Solving Olympiad Geometry without Human Demonstrations*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06747-5). AlphaGeometry; a system combining neural methods and symbolic deduction.
- **Empirical (scientific)** · **Jumper et al. (2021).** [*Highly Accurate Protein Structure Prediction with AlphaFold*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03819-2). A landmark example of machine learning enabling scientific prediction.
- **Primary technical** · **Preskill (2018).** [*Quantum Computing in the NISQ Era and Beyond*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00862). Explains opportunities and limitations of near-term quantum computation, and why fault tolerance matters.

These works are evidence of progress in AI-assisted scientific computation and mathematical problem-solving, **not evidence that a revolution in fundamental theoretical physics will necessarily occur within 20 years**.
