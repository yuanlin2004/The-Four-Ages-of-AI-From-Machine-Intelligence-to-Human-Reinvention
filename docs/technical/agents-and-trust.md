# Technical companion · Agents, software and trust

*From application-centered software to distributed intelligence, trusted execution and human agency*

!!! note "A technical companion to the essay"
    This companion was originally the essay's third chapter, *The Age of Agentic Computing*. It describes the systems through which people may delegate decisions, the mechanism behind **[Age II · The Surrender](../age-2-surrender.md)**, and the economics of generated software that feed **[Age III · The Irrelevance](../age-3-irrelevance.md)**.

Agentic computing is not merely the addition of tool-calling agents to large language models. It is a broader redesign of computing around systems that can interpret objectives and act on behalf of people.

Traditional applications require users to understand predefined interfaces. An agent can instead accept an objective, decompose the work, invoke tools, manage state and verify results. Applications become resources and capabilities that intelligent systems coordinate.

## Personal computing returns to the center

Today the industry emphasizes large cloud models, but personal devices are becoming important sites of inference. Model weights, KV caches, retrieval and contextual state introduce new performance demands on memory bandwidth, capacity and data locality.

CPUs, GPUs and AI accelerators are likely to become more tightly coupled through heterogeneous architectures and shared memory. They need not literally merge into one homogeneous processor: their strengths remain different. But software may increasingly schedule work across them without exposing those differences to users.

The operating system may acquire new responsibilities: persistent agent state, contextual memory, model scheduling, identity management and fine-grained permissions. Files and applications will remain, but a personal computing environment may increasingly be organized around ongoing tasks and knowledge rather than isolated app sessions.

## Local intelligence plus cloud intelligence

A useful architecture will often be hybrid. Local models can offer lower latency, privacy, availability and control. Cloud models can offer greater computational capacity. Tasks may be allocated dynamically among on-device, edge and centralized resources.

This creates a network of agents and models interacting with tools, enterprise systems and each other. The network becomes not just a transport for information but also a medium through which independently acting systems coordinate authority and work.

## The generative software economy

Vibe coding foreshadows a fall in the cost of translating requirements into software. As coding agents improve, people may create custom workflows and interfaces instead of selecting the nearest fit from a SaaS catalog.

This challenges application-centered SaaS economics and pricing based primarily on user seats. But an AI-generated application is not automatically an enterprise-ready system. Long-term maintenance, data integrity, compliance, integration, security and operations remain difficult.

The durable advantages of software vendors may increasingly depend on authoritative data, distribution, transaction networks, domain expertise, trusted execution and measurable outcomes.

<div class="pull-quote" markdown>
When code becomes inexpensive to generate, **trusted operation** becomes comparatively more valuable.
</div>

## Security and the agency boundary

An autonomous agent may read external content, select tools, execute transactions and delegate tasks. It cannot safely be granted broad, indefinite authority simply because its human user has those privileges.

A dependable agentic infrastructure needs scoped credentials, explicit approvals for consequential actions, isolated execution, replayable audit trails, reliable retries, idempotency and recovery from partial failure.

It must also distinguish **untrusted information** from instructions that have legitimate authority. Documents, emails, web pages and other agents may offer evidence, but they should not silently rewrite the system's goals or permissions.

As agents proliferate, so will generated messages, code and documents. Information grows cheaper to produce; provenance and verification become scarcer.

## The human question: assistance or surrender?

Delegating routine work can expand human freedom. Delegating judgment without understanding its consequences can reduce autonomy. Who defines an agent's objective? Who approves irreversible actions? Who is accountable when its interpretation of a goal conflicts with the user's values?

There is a parallel economic question. Work provides income, but it also provides identity, meaning and social participation. If machines perform more economically valuable cognitive work, society may have to reconsider how it allocates opportunity and recognition.

Human dignity is not reducible to labor-market productivity. Yet our institutions frequently behave as though the two were tightly linked.

Agentic computing is therefore a challenge to technical infrastructure **and** the institutions governing human agency.
